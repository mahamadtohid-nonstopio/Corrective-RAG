# Hypertension Management — JNC 8 / 2017 ACC/AHA Combined Notes

## Diagnostic thresholds (2017 ACC/AHA)

- Normal: < 120/80 mmHg
- Elevated: 120–129/< 80 mmHg
- Stage 1 hypertension: 130–139/80–89 mmHg
- Stage 2 hypertension: ≥ 140/90 mmHg

## Treatment thresholds and targets

- Adults with 10-year ASCVD risk ≥ 10% or clinical CVD, CKD, or diabetes:
  start pharmacotherapy at ≥ 130/80 mmHg.
- Otherwise start pharmacotherapy at ≥ 140/90 mmHg.
- Target: < 130/80 mmHg for most adults.
- Age ≥ 65 without polypharmacy concerns: < 130 mmHg SBP target is reasonable.

## First-line agents (non-Black)

- Thiazide diuretic (e.g., chlorthalidone 12.5–25 mg)
- ACE inhibitor (e.g., lisinopril)
- ARB (e.g., losartan)
- Long-acting calcium channel blocker (e.g., amlodipine)

## First-line agents (Black adults without HF or CKD)

- Thiazide diuretic or long-acting CCB.
- ACEi/ARB not preferred as monotherapy due to reduced BP response, but indicated
  if CKD or HF is present.

## Chronic kidney disease

For adults with CKD and hypertension, first-line therapy should include an
ACE inhibitor or ARB, regardless of race, when albuminuria is present.

## Resistant hypertension

- Confirmed BP ≥ 130/80 mmHg despite ≥ 3 optimally dosed antihypertensives
  (including a diuretic).
- Add spironolactone 12.5–25 mg daily as fourth-line agent if K+ and eGFR permit.
