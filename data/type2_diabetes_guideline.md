# ADA Standards of Care 2024 — Pharmacologic Therapy for Type 2 Diabetes (Excerpt)

## First-line therapy

Metformin, together with comprehensive lifestyle modification (nutrition, physical
activity, weight management, smoking cessation), remains the preferred initial
pharmacologic agent for most adults with type 2 diabetes without atherosclerotic
cardiovascular disease (ASCVD), heart failure (HF), or chronic kidney disease
(CKD). Titrate to 2000 mg/day as tolerated. (Evidence level A.)

## Patients with established or high risk of ASCVD

In adults with type 2 diabetes and established ASCVD or at high cardiovascular
risk, a GLP-1 receptor agonist with proven CV benefit (e.g., semaglutide,
liraglutide, dulaglutide) or an SGLT2 inhibitor with proven CV benefit
(e.g., empagliflozin, canagliflozin) is recommended as part of the glucose-
lowering regimen, independent of baseline A1C and metformin use. (Evidence A.)

## Patients with heart failure

An SGLT2 inhibitor (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin) is recommended to reduce risk
of hospitalisation for HF and cardiovascular death, regardless of ejection
fraction. (Evidence A.)

## Patients with CKD

For adults with type 2 diabetes and CKD with eGFR 20–60 mL/min/1.73 m^2 and/or
albuminuria, an SGLT2 inhibitor with proven kidney benefit
(empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, or canagliflozin) is preferred to reduce
progression of CKD and cardiovascular events. Add a non-steroidal mineralocorticoid
receptor antagonist (finerenone) in adults with type 2 diabetes and CKD with
albuminuria and eGFR ≥25 mL/min/1.73 m^2 who are on maximally tolerated
ACE inhibitor or ARB, to reduce cardiovascular events and CKD progression.
(Evidence A.)

## Metformin considerations in CKD

Metformin may be continued at eGFR ≥30 mL/min/1.73 m^2 with dose reduction below
45. Discontinue if eGFR < 30. Reassess renal function at least annually.

## A1C targets

Individualised A1C target of < 7.0% is reasonable for most non-pregnant adults.
Consider less stringent targets (< 8.0%) for patients with limited life expectancy,
history of severe hypoglycaemia, or advanced complications.
