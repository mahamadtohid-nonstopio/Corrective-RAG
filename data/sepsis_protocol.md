# Surviving Sepsis Campaign — Adult Management Bundle (Summary)

## Hour-1 bundle

Within 1 hour of sepsis / septic shock recognition:

1. Measure lactate. Re-measure if initial lactate > 2 mmol/L.
2. Obtain blood cultures BEFORE administering antibiotics (do not delay antibiotics
   beyond 45 minutes if cultures cannot be obtained promptly).
3. Administer broad-spectrum antibiotics covering likely pathogens.
4. Begin rapid crystalloid administration at 30 mL/kg for hypotension or lactate
   ≥ 4 mmol/L. Reassess after each 500 mL bolus.
5. Apply vasopressors if hypotensive during or after fluid resuscitation to
   maintain MAP ≥ 65 mmHg. Norepinephrine is the first-line vasopressor.

## Ongoing resuscitation

- Target MAP ≥ 65 mmHg (higher targets in previously hypertensive patients if
  perfusion improves).
- Guide fluid therapy with dynamic measures (passive leg raise, stroke-volume
  variation) rather than static CVP after initial resuscitation.
- Add vasopressin (0.03 U/min) as second-line vasopressor to reduce
  norepinephrine dose or when MAP inadequate on norepinephrine alone.
- Add hydrocortisone 200 mg/day IV in patients with refractory septic shock on
  ongoing vasopressor therapy.

## Antimicrobial stewardship

- Reassess antibiotics daily. De-escalate based on cultures and clinical response.
- Duration: typical 7–10 days for uncomplicated sepsis; extend for slow responders,
  undrainable foci, or specific pathogens (e.g., S. aureus bacteraemia).

## Source control

Identify and control the infection source (e.g., abscess drainage, device removal,
surgical intervention) as soon as medically and logistically feasible, ideally
within 6–12 hours of diagnosis.
