# Atrial Fibrillation — Anticoagulation Guidance (Summary)

## Stroke-risk stratification

- Use CHA2DS2-VASc for non-valvular AF.
- Men score ≥ 2, women score ≥ 3: oral anticoagulation recommended.
- Men score 1, women score 2: oral anticoagulation may be considered.
- Score 0 (men) or 1 (women): no antithrombotic therapy is preferred.

## Bleeding-risk assessment

- Use HAS-BLED. A score ≥ 3 flags high bleeding risk; do NOT withhold
  anticoagulation on this basis alone. Address modifiable factors (uncontrolled BP,
  concurrent NSAIDs/alcohol, labile INR).

## Anticoagulant selection

- For non-valvular AF, direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) — apixaban, rivaroxaban,
  dabigatran, edoxaban — are preferred over warfarin for most patients.
- Warfarin remains preferred for:
  - Mechanical heart valves.
  - Moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis (rheumatic).
  - eGFR < 15 mL/min/1.73 m^2 (with caution) or dialysis (individualise).

## Renal dose adjustments (adults)

- Apixaban 5 mg BID; reduce to 2.5 mg BID if ≥ 2 of: age ≥ 80, weight ≤ 60 kg,
  serum creatinine ≥ 1.5 mg/dL.
- Rivaroxaban 20 mg once daily with evening meal; reduce to 15 mg if CrCl
  15–50 mL/min. Avoid if CrCl < 15.
- Dabigatran 150 mg BID; 75 mg BID if CrCl 15–30 mL/min. Avoid if CrCl < 15.

## Peri-procedural interruption

- Low bleeding risk procedures: continue DOAC.
- Standard bleeding risk: hold DOAC 1–2 days pre-procedure (longer for
  dabigatran if CrCl low). Resume 24 h post-procedure once haemostasis is achieved.
